### Guided timed sets

For exercises where duration is the work, North could keep the user on the workout screen instead of forcing them to open another timer app. This is most useful for dead hangs, planks, wall sits, isometric holds, carries, mobility positions and sequences with several timed positions.

The experience should be prescription-aware rather than adding a stopwatch to every exercise:

- A large, distance-readable countdown replaces small set text while the hold is active.
- Strong colour changes identify work, transition, next position, rest and completion without requiring the user to read the screen closely.
- Multi-position exercises can show the next position before automatically advancing to its timer.
- Optional sound and haptic cues support users who cannot keep looking at the phone.
- Pause, extend, skip and restart remain available without leaving the workout.
- Completed duration is saved as part of the set record.
- Rep-based work such as squats remains untimed by default; a timer appears only when duration or a coach-authored prescription makes it useful.

The phone should work when propped against a water bottle several feet away: the current phase and remaining time must be legible at a glance. The first useful test is a single timed hold and a simple two-position sequence, not a general interval-training engine.

Why it waits:

North first needs reliable duration-based exercise prescriptions and set records. The interaction also needs real gym-floor testing for screen wake lock, background behavior, sound, haptics and accessibility before it can be trusted during a set.

### Coach workspace and business accounts

North could connect the work a client records between appointments with the coach who is helping them progress.

The client would keep a normal private North account, record sessions and own their data. They could invite or approve a coach, choose what the coach may see and remove access at any time. A coach could maintain a private client list, review completed sessions and trends, leave feedback, prepare or adjust training and see where attention is needed before the next appointment.

Potential business model:

- Clients subscribe to the standard North membership; $14.99 is an early pricing hypothesis, not a committed price.
- Coaches pay separately for a professional workspace, with pricing based on active-client capacity or practice size.
- A coach who brings an eligible new client to North could receive a one-time $4.99 referral reward after the client's first paid $14.99 month clears its refund window.
- A coach subscription pays for professional workflow and oversight, not ownership of client accounts or health data.
- Later tiers could support assistant coaches, shared practices and branded invitations without creating a public marketplace.

Trust boundaries:

- Every coach-client connection requires explicit client consent.
- Clients control data categories and can revoke access immediately.
- Private reflections, Nova conversations and health data remain hidden unless separately and clearly shared.
- Coach views and changes are auditable; plan changes never silently overwrite a client's record.
- North does not claim that a coach has reviewed something unless the coach actually has.

Why it waits:

The individual workout, account, sync, privacy and progress loops must be excellent first. Coach mode amplifies a trusted personal product; it cannot compensate for an unfinished one.
